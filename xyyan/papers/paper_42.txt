SUMMARY
A brief exposure of murine peritoneal inflammatory macrophages to plasma histidine-rich
glycoprotein (HRG; 77,000-81,000 MW) for 1-2 hr increased Fcy receptor (FcyR) expression and
phagocytic function in thesecells. However, acontinualculture ofthecellswithoutthepresenceof
HRGforthenext 18-48hrresultedindown-regulationofFcyRexpressionandphagocyticfunction.
Similarly, HRG decreased FcyRII expression in less differentiated human THP-1 monocytic cells
duringtreatmentfor 18hr,asdeterminedbycellularELISAandmetaboliclabelling.Themolecular
mechanism by which HRG regulates FcyR expression is unknown. However, at a relatively high
concentration (> pg/ml), HRG altered the cellular metabolism by increasing cellular protein
1
synthesis but reducing protein secretion. These observations suggest a likely mechanism for the
HRG-mediatedreductionofFcyRexpression. Adegraded HRG (40,000 MW)which possessed an
identical N-terminal sequence as that ofthe native HRG was capable ofdecreasing macrophage
FcyR expression and phagocytosis. The results indicate that the functional domain of HRG
responsible forbinding to macrophages is localized to the N-terminal half.
