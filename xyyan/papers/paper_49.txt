Histidine-rich Glycoprotein Modulates the Blood-vascular
System in Septic Condition
Hidenori Wake＊§
Department of Pharmacology, Okayama University Graduate School of Medicine, Dentistry,
and Pharmaceutical Sciences, Okayama 700-8558, Japan
Histidine-rich glycoprotein (HRG) is a 75kDa glycoprotein synthesized in the liver whose plasma concentra-
tion is 100-150μg/ml. HRG has been shown to modulate sepsis-related biological reactions by binding to sev-
eral substances and cells, including heparin, factor XII, fibrinogen, thrombospondin, plasminogen, C1q,
IgG, heme, LPS, dead cells, bacteria, and fungi. Therefore, reduction of plasma HRG levels in sepsis leads to
dysregulation of coagulation, fibrinolysis, and immune response, resulting in disseminated intravascular coag-
ulation and multiple organ failure. This review summarizes the binding and functional properties of HRG in
sepsis.
