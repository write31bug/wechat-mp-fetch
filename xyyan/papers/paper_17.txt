HRG attenuates DNA- or RNA-induced activation of
FXII
Having confirmed that nucleic acids promote clotting via the in-
trinsic pathway, we next set out to identify the reactions that are
modulated by HRG. The effect of HRG on DNA- or RNA-induced
activation of FXII was examined in the presence of PK and HK be-
cause nucleic acids enhanced activation of FXII up to 180-fold in
the presence of HK and PK, but only four-fold in their absence
(▶ Table 1). HRG attenuated FXIIa generation in a concentration-
dependent fashion producing up to 95 % inhibition with an EC of
50
about 200 nM (▶ Figure 3 A). Therefore, by binding FXIIa, HRG
attenuates DNA- or RNA-induced activation of FXII; findings
consistent with previous results obtained with dextran sulfate (22).

HRG displaces FXI bound to DNA or RNA
To determine whether HRG modulates thrombin activation of FXI
by binding to the nucleic acid template, we used SPR to assess the
capacity of HRG to displace DNA or RNA bound to immobilised
FXI. The experiment was designed to exploit the facts that DNA
and RNA dissociation from FXI is slow, and that HRG does not
bind FXI (22). DNA and RNA bound immobilised FXI in a satu-
rable manner (▶ Figure 6) and subsequent injection of HRG accel-
erated their dissociation in a concentration-dependent and satu-
rable manner. Therefore, by binding to the nucleic acids, HRG in-
hibited FXI binding. These results provide evidence for competi-
tive binding of HRG and FXI to nucleic acids, thereby identifying a
secondary mechanism through which HRG regulates the contact
pathway of coagulation.
