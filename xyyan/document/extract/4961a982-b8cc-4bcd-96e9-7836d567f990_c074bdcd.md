## Abstract

The plasma protein histidine-rich glycoprotein (HRG) affects the morphology and function of both endothelial cells (ECs) and monocytes/macrophages in cancer. Here, we examined the mechanism of action of HRG's effect on ECs. HRG suppressed adhesion, spreading and migration of ECs specifically on collagen I (COL I) whereas ECs seeded on other extracellular matrix proteins were insensitive to HRG. HRG did not bind specifically to COL I or to the  $ \alpha $ -integrin binding site on collagen, GFOGER. Furthermore, HRG's inhibition of EC adhesion was not dependent upon heparan sulfate (HS) moieties as heparinase-treated ECs remained sensitive to HRG. C2C12 cells expressing  $ \alpha $ 2 integrin, the major collagen-binding  $ \alpha $ -integrin subunit in ECs, showed increased binding of HRG compared with wild type C2C12 cells lacking the  $ \alpha $ 2 subunit. Recombinant  $ \alpha $ 2 I-domain protein bound HRG and to a higher extent when in active conformation. However, the  $ \alpha $ 2 I-domain bound weakly to HRG compared to COL I and the purified  $ \alpha $ 2 $ \beta $ 1 ectodomain complex failed to retain HRG. We conclude that HRG binds to  $ \alpha $ 2 integrin through low-affinity interactions in a HS-independent manner, thereby blocking EC-adhesion to COL I.

HRG, adhesion, integrin, endothelial cells, collagen.

### 1. Introduction

Histidine-rich glycoprotein (HRG) is a 73 kDa heparan sulfate (HS)-binding glycoprotein present in plasma at 150  $ \mu $ g/ml [1]. Hrg gene inactivation in mice is compatible with embryonic development and adult fertility, but gives rise to increased clot formation as well as increased fibrinolysis [2]. Reports from our and other laboratories strongly implicate mononuclear cells in HRG biology. HRG induces gene regulation changes in monocytes/macrophages [3] and iodinated HRG binds specifically to monocytic cells in a HS-independent manner [4].

Endothelial cells (ECs) and monocytic cells communicate in physiological and pathological processes e.g. by reciprocal exchange of growth modulatory factors  $ [5, 6] $ . HRG and HRG-derived peptides have been shown to affect ECs, e.g. by suppressing endothelial chemotaxis towards growth factors  $ [7, 8] $ . Moreover, ECs in the vasculature of HRG-expressing tumors attain a more mature morphology than non-HRG expressing tumors  $ [9] $ . By binding and neutralization of proteins with anti-angiogenic properties such as thrombospondin and vasculastatin  $ [10, 11] $ , HRG may also indirectly, support angiogenesis.

The mode of action of HRG in affecting ECs has remained unclear. We therefore asked whether HRG acts directly on endothelial cells, and if so, through which mechanism. We show here that HRG suppressed EC attachment and spreading on type-I collagen (COL I), in a HS-independent manner. In contrast, EC attachment to other matrices was not affected by HRG. HRG bound to integrin  $ \alpha_{2} $ -expressing cells and bound with low efficiency to the integrin  $ \alpha_{2} $  protein I-domain, but not to the purified  $ \alpha_{2}\beta_{1} $  integrin ectodomain complex. We conclude that HRG's ability to interfere with EC adhesion and motility is critically dependent on the presence of  $ \alpha_{2} $  integrin, despite the lack of high-affinity interaction between the two proteins. The interplay may require other molecular partners yet to be identified. The data may have a bearing on the interpretation of HRG's effect on suppressing cancer growth and spread in vivo, which in part may depend on a primary effect of HRG on ECs.

### 2. Results

### 2.1 HRG negatively regulates endothelial cell migration and adhesion to COL I

HRG and peptides derived from HRG have previously been shown to inhibit endothelial cell migration in vitro  $ [7, 8] $ . We sought to determine the underlying mechanism. First, we confirmed that with the current experimental set-up, purified, recombinant human HRG indeed inhibited migration of human umbilical vein ECs (HUVECs) through a COL I-coated membrane (Figure 1A). The effect of HRG was comparable to that of an integrin  $ \alpha_{2} $ -neutralizing antibody, which blocks adhesion of cells to COL I  $ [12] $ . Moreover, HRG inhibited HUVEC adhesion to COL I-coated wells (Figure 1B).

To further explore HRG's modulation of HUVEC adhesion, we examined the potential matrix dependence in HRG's inhibition of HUVEC adhesion. As shown in Figure 1C, HRG inhibited HUVEC adhesion to COL I, but not to laminin, fibronectin or vitronectin. The specificity for HRG's inhibition of adhesion to COL I, but not to fibronectin, was recapitulated using the xCELLigence system, which measures current impedance changes following cell adherence and spreading on matrix (Figure 1D, E). The observation that HRG inhibits EC adhesion to COL I is particularly interesting as the localization of HRG in T241 fibrosarcomas tissue often occurred around collagen-rich vascular structures identified by

immunostaining for the vascular adhesion protein CD31 (Figure 2). Thus, HRG's COL I-specificity may have a significant in vivo role in modulating endothelial cell biology.

### 2.2 HRG does not directly alter HUVEC gene expression

We previously demonstrated that HRG affects expression of macrophage genes involved in the regulation of the extracellular matrix and immune responsiveness  $ [3] $ . To examine whether a similar gene regulation pattern was observed in HRG-treated HUVECs, transcript arrays were performed. Of the 29,000 genes examined, no significant changes in gene expression were found when comparing HRG- and buffer-treated HUVECs at either 6 or 24 hours after addition of HRG at 1 ug/ml (GEO Accession no GSE66768). This demonstrated that HRG's effects on HUVECs are not due to changes in gene expression over the time frames analyzed, consistent with the observation that HRG's inhibition of HUVEC adhesion can be seen in relatively short duration assays.

### 2.3 HRG does not bind specifically to COL I

We next sought to determine whether the specificity of HRG's inhibitory effects on EC adhesion and spreading on COL I was due to a specific binding of HRG to the matrix. We incubated various matrices with HRG and quantified bound HRG by ELISA  $ [4] $ . While HRG inhibited cell adhesion only to COL I, it bound in a dose-dependent manner to all matrices tested compared to BSA-blocked wells (Figure 3A). However, the promiscuity of HRG's matrix interactions did not rule out the possibility that HRG might bind to different motifs in different matrices allowing specific interference with adhesion e.g. to COL I. To examine this possibility, we tested the ability of HRG to bind to the triple helical peptide GFOGER, the integrin-binding motif of COL I  $ [12] $  (Figure 3B). HRG did however not display a specific interaction with GFOGER compared to the control peptides GPP and GPO, which have a triple-helix organization similar to that of GFOGER. Therefore, HRG's ability to inhibit endothelial cell adhesion to COL I was likely not related to matrix interactions. This suggests that HRG's inhibition of adhesion occurs on the integrin side of the integrin-matrix interaction, which is in line with our observation that pre-incubation of endothelial cells with HRG significantly enhanced adhesion blockade (Figure 3C). Moreover, when treating HUVECs with HRG followed by a wash-step, the inhibitory effect of HRG on HUVEC adhesion to COL I disappeared (Figure 3D). These results indicate HRG binds with low affinity to the EC surface and that HRG's effects on adhesion are not mediated via cell signalling changes.

### 2.4 HRG's inhibition of EC adhesion to COL I is independent of HS proteoglycan interactions

Binding to HS is known to facilitate and stabilize cell surface receptor-ligand interactions. Specifically, the HS-proteoglycan syndecan-1 supports high affinity binding of  $ \alpha_{2} $  integrin to collagen. This mechanism seems to be independent of integrin conformational activation or direct binding of syndecan to collagen [13]. Consequently, loss of syndecan-1 results in a lower binding affinity and a reduction of  $ \alpha_{2}\beta_{1} $ -mediated collagen adhesion.
