## Key Findings
Sepsis-induced endothelial barrier dysfunction involves circulating lipopolysaccharide (LPS), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and other mediators that activate endothelial heparanase, triggering the enzymatic shedding of the endothelial glycocalyx (EG). In sepsis, reciprocal activation between endothelial cells and leukocytes amplifies inflammation into a self-sustaining loop. Future sepsis research will focus on how metabolic reprogramming of endothelial cells uses specific metabolites to modulate inflammation and barrier function, the direct impact of gut microbial translocation on endothelial phenotype, and mechanisms governing endothelial repair and regeneration.

## Clinical Significance
Elucidates key mechanisms of endothelial barrier dysfunction in sepsis, opening new avenues for precision therapeutics targeting the endothelial glycocalyx and inflammatory pathways to improve patient outcomes.
