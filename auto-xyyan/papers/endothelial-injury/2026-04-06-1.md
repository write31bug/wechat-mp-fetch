## Key Findings
The study reveals that glycolysis-derived H3K14 lactylation (H3K14la) plays a key role in endothelial dysfunction by regulating the transcription of ferroptosis-related genes, including transferrin receptor and solute carrier family 40 member 1 (SLC40A1). Ferroptosis inhibition using ferrostatin-1 or deferoxamine significantly reduced endothelial cell activation, mitigating sepsis-induced vascular injury and inflammation. The study establishes the glycolysis/H3K14la/ferroptosis axis as a key pathway in sepsis-induced endothelial dysfunction.

## Clinical Significance
Identifies a novel therapeutic target (the glycolysis/H3K14la/ferroptosis axis) for treating sepsis-associated ARDS, suggesting that targeting this pathway could provide a novel therapeutic approach for treating sepsis-associated ARDS and reducing vascular injury.
