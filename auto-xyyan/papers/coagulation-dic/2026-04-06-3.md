## Key Findings
This article outlines the mechanisms of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) and ferroptosis, discusses the role of NETs in myocardial injury and the importance of ferroptosis in sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy, and reviews the potential interconnection between these two types of programmed cell death in sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy. Neutrophil extracellular traps mediate m(6)A modification and regulate sepsis-associated acute lung injury by activating ferroptosis in alveolar epithelial cells. Mesenchymal stem cells alleviate sepsis-induced acute lung injury by blocking neutrophil extracellular traps formation and inhibiting ferroptosis in rats.

## Clinical Significance
Highlights the interconnected roles of NETosis and ferroptosis in sepsis-induced organ damage, particularly cardiomyopathy and lung injury, providing potential therapeutic targets for reducing organ dysfunction in septic patients.
