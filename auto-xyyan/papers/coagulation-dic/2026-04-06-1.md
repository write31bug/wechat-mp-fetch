## Key Findings
In 2025, the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) released updated definitions and diagnostic criteria for disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), reflecting advances in understanding its pathophysiology. Early-phase DIC (also referred to as subclinical or compensated DIC) is characterized by laboratory abnormalities preceding clinical symptoms. Overt DIC represents the advanced stage with clear evidence of coagulopathy and organ failure. Thrombotic DIC is marked by microvascular thrombosis and organ dysfunction, while hemorrhagic DIC is characterized by bleeding due to consumption of coagulation factors.

## Clinical Significance
Provides updated diagnostic criteria for DIC that reflect current understanding of pathophysiology, enabling earlier detection and more targeted treatment approaches for patients with sepsis-induced coagulopathy.
